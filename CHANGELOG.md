# Changelog

All notable changes to this package are documented here. This project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- How a person checks each clause by hand: with core 1.15 and later, `proceduresFor('9.1.4.3', { packs: [en301549], standard: 'en301549', version: 'V4.1.1' })` gives the procedures of the WCAG criterion the clause restates, in both versions and every language core has them. The pack gives them from its clause table through core's kit, with no text of its own; an older core gives none and ignores them.
- How a person assesses EN 301 549's own requirements by hand, the ones that restate no WCAG criterion: with core 1.15 and later, `proceduresFor('4.2.1', { packs: [en301549], standard: 'en301549', version: 'V4.1.1' })` reads them in the shape of a clause's. They cover chapter 4's functional performance statements (`4.2.1` to `4.2.11`), chapter 12 (`12.1.1` to `12.2.4` in V3.2.1, `12.1` to `12.5` in V4.1.1, which renumbered it) and chapters 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 13 by number (`'5'`), in both versions, each with the version's own title. A chapter 4 statement sends the person to the chapter 9 clauses Annex B relates to it and adds whole tasks done the way a person with that need does them; a chapter starts by deciding whether it applies. They are in English, as core gives a pack's own procedures, and add no rule, rollup, profile entry or result: with an older core the pack gives what it gave before.
- How a person assesses chapter 9's own requirements, beside its clauses: with core 1.15 and later, `proceduresFor('9.6', …)` gives WCAG's five conformance requirements at Level AA, in both versions, and `proceduresFor('9.7', { …, version: 'V4.1.1' })` the user's preferences for web pages, new in V4.1.1: the page's colours, text size, pointer and text cursor follow the user's settings, unless that is essential. 9.6 relates to the criteria of the four clauses its fifth conformance requirement asks of all content (`relatesTo`). In English, as the standard's other own requirements are.
- The standard's chapters, for reading it whole: with core 1.15 and later, `listProcedures({ packs: [en301549], standard: 'en301549' })` and `procedureChecklist` give every requirement of a version in its chapters, chapter 4's statements, chapter 12's requirements and each other chapter, with chapter 9's clauses under the principles and guidelines they are numbered by (9.1 Perceivable, 9.1.1 Text alternatives), then its own 9.6 and 9.7. Every requirement is in exactly one group (`groups.js`).
- Each chapter 4 statement names the WCAG criteria it relates to (`relatesTo`). In V4.1.1 they are those restated by every chapter 9 clause Table B.2 marks P (primary) for the criterion, in number order; 9.6 and 9.7 restate none. Secondary marks are left out on purpose, as Annex B defines them as partial support ("some users may use the feature in specific situations"), while the procedure still sends the person to every P and S clause, as Annex C's first check asks. In V3.2.1, whose Annex B is not recorded, they are those of the chapter 9 clauses its procedure names. With core 1.15 and later, `relatedRequirements('1.4.3', { packs: [en301549] })` gives 4.2.2 and 4.2.10 next to 9.1.4.3, and `relatedRequirements('4.2.2', { packs: [en301549], standard: 'en301549' })` its criteria.
- Annex B of V4.1.1 for the clause 4.2 criteria, in `docs/annex-b-v4.1.1.md`: every clause Table B.2 marks P or S for 4.2.1 to 4.2.11, in every chapter, with its page, the chapter 9 clauses the procedures name, and Annex C's test for each criterion. The cells that could not be read cleanly were checked against the page images: the LR cells of 9.4.1.3, 10.4.1.3 and 11.4.1.3 print "-P", which the table doesn't define, so they are in neither list for 4.2.8, and the empty cells are no relationship. No list changed.
- Table B.2 of V4.1.1 as data, in `annex-b.js`: each clause 4.2 criterion with its Table B.1 column code and the clauses marked P and S. Tests keep it the same as `docs/annex-b-v4.1.1.md`.

- The standard in French, German, Spanish and Japanese as well as English, with core 1.15 and later: its own procedures, its clauses', chapters' and groups' titles and V4.1.1's note, keyed by the English they translate (`standard.lang: 'en'` and `standard.translations`, from `i18n/standard.<locale>.json`). Read in one of them, `listProcedures`, `procedureChecklist`, `proceduresFor` and a page made from them are in that language throughout, core's procedures of the chapter 9 clauses included, and a scan names the clauses in it; in a language the standard doesn't have, they are all in English. Clause titles follow UNE-EN 301549 in Spanish and DIN EN 301549 in German where those give them, and chapter 9's are WCAG's criteria as core names them. An older core ignores the translations.

### Changed

- V4.1.1's own procedures were reviewed against the published standard. Chapter 4 is "Functional performance criteria", and each criterion's related clauses are those Annex B marks primary or secondary, in any chapter, with the second route Annex C allows: evidence that the outcome is achieved. Chapter 6 is assessed clause by clause, in five procedures (voice, real-time text, caller identification, video, total conversation) with the limits V4.1.1 sets, such as 500 ms for real-time text and 20 frames per second for video. Chapter 10 has procedures for documents as clause 10 words the criteria, for sets of documents (10.3.2.3, 10.3.2.4 and 10.3.2.6) and for the reader's display settings (10.7). Closed functionality (5.1.3 and 5.1.8), operable parts found by touch, authoring tools for chapter 11's content, subtitles within 100 ms and switched in one operation, chapter 8's dimensions and clauses 8.6 to 8.8, the void clauses of chapter 11 and its platform tools, chapter 13's 13.1.2 and 13.1.3, and the platform differences of 9.7 follow the standard's wording. V3.2.1's are unchanged.
- The procedures name the tools they suggest: for documents (chapter 10), Adobe Acrobat Reader and PAC; for non-web software (chapter 11), the platforms' inspection tools: Accessibility Inspector in Xcode, Accessibility Scanner and the layout inspector, and Accessibility Insights.
- V4.1.1's procedures follow its wording for 4.2.9, 5.7 and 5.8. 4.2.9 fails when the mode the product starts in flashes, and audiovisual material comes with no warning: choosing a safe mode isn't enough. 5.7 applies where key repeat can't be turned off, and asks for both the delay and the repeat rate. 5.8 asks that the time in which the same key isn't accepted again can be set up to at least 0.5 seconds. V3.2.1's are unchanged.
- The V3.2.1 related-clauses step lists the chapter 9 clauses Table B.2 marks P or S, as its pass condition and the V4.1.1 step already count.
- In V4.1.1, 4.2.6, 4.2.8 and 4.2.11 have a related-clauses facet like the other criteria's, for columns WVC, LR and PR, before their own; 4.2.11's step that sent the person to Annex B is part of it now. Every V4.1.1 related-clauses facet names its examples from the clauses Table B.2 marks P for the criterion, chapter 9 first, and asks for those of chapters 5 to 13 that apply. V3.2.1's are unchanged.
- With core 1.15 and later, a V4.1.1 list of procedures says in its header which edition is cited where, and that Annex C gives each clause's formal test (`standard.notes`), and lists 9.4.1.1 Parsing as void, with why, so a tester coming from V3.2.1 doesn't look for it.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-10-26

Needs `@surea11y/core` 1.14.0 for the changes below; with 1.13 the pack behaves as 1.0.1 does, as 1.13 ignores the options it now gives.

### Fixed

- Under the `en301549-v3.2.1` profile, a duplicate id keeps failing 9.4.1.1 with core 1.14. Core 1.14 follows the 2023 errata to WCAG 2.0 and 2.1, which count 4.1.1 Parsing as satisfied, unless the profile says its standard keeps the criterion's text; V3.2.1 does, and its profile now says so (`keepsParsing: true`). Without it, core 1.14 asks about a duplicate id there instead of failing it, and warns once that the profile loses 4.1.1.

### Changed

- A clause's result is `notApplicable` only where the WCAG rollup of the criterion it restates is, as core 1.14.0 judges WCAG's (`applicability: 'wcag'`): where core's rules don't see every case the criterion applies to and all of them found nothing, the clause is `cantTell` (`composite.rollup.cantTell.partialCoverage`), so 9.1.2.2 agrees with WCAG 1.2.2 in the same report. Clauses about links, form fields, focusable elements, labelled components, pointer targets and markup stay `notApplicable` when there is nothing to check. Under `mappings: ['en301549']`, the WCAG rollups name the same clauses as before.
- No clause passes with core 1.14, as no WCAG criterion does: core's rules check only part of what each criterion asks, so a clause whose rules all pass is `cantTell` (`composite.rollup.cantTell.partialCheck`), for a person to check the rest.
- Developed and tested against core 1.14.0. Its new rules check their clauses with no change here, as the map is derived from core's catalog, and `docs/RULE_CATALOG.md` lists them: 9.1.4.5, 9.3.3.1 and 9.4.1.3 get their first rules (`image-of-text`, `invalid-field-error-message`, `live-region-announcements`), and `click-handler-keyboard-operable`, `widget-keyboard-focusable`, `focus-not-removed-by-script`, `moving-content-pausable`, `aria-owns-valid` and `aria-expanded-state-mismatch` join the clauses of their criteria.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-19

### Added

- EN 301 549 chapter 9 (Web) as a pack for `@surea11y/core`, taken from core, which had it built in up to 1.12: the clause table for V3.2.1 and V4.1.1, published as `@surea11y/pack-en301549/public` with the exports `@surea11y/core/en301549` had; the clauses each of core's rules checks, derived from its WCAG criteria; `engineOptions.mappings: ['en301549']` (or `'en301549:V3.2.1'`, `'en301549:V4.1.1'`), which names on each result the entries core 1.12.1 named; one result per clause; and the profiles `en301549-v4.1.1` and `en301549-v3.2.1`, which run only the rules that check one of the version's clauses and report its clauses alone, judged by WCAG 2.2 and 2.1. Needs core 1.13.0 or later, where a scan given the pack uses it in place of core's deprecated built-in.
